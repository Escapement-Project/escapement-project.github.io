Verge

Open CMSIS-DAP firmware for NXP's MCU-Link and MCU-Link Pro

Verge takes the place of NXP's firmware on the probe. Any CMSIS-DAP host drives it, as it drives NXP's.

Through probe-rs, Verge is faster than NXP's firmware at every clock both complete. It speaks cJTAG, which NXP's firmware doesn't. And it reads right at clocks where NXP's firmware misreads.

Verge generates the SWD and JTAG transfer code at runtime for the clock you ask for. At a fast clock it calibrates read sampling against your target when it attaches. One image runs on both probes.

{% if ",getting-started," in written %}Install and update{% else %}Install and update{% endif %}

With verge, a command line and a desktop app for Linux, Windows and macOS

  1. Get verge

    Download the archive for your system from verge's releases. Each holds the command line and the desktop app, with a checksum and a signature.

  2. See what is attached

    $verge list

    Each MCU-Link, the firmware it runs, and how it can update.

  3. Update

    $verge update

    The latest release, its signature checked first. A probe running NXP's firmware from V3.122 needs no jumper.

To put NXP's firmware back, verge update --stock installs it from your LinkServer installation. {% if ",getting-started," in written %}Getting started{% else %}Getting started{% endif %} has the details.

As an analyser recorded them at 1 ns, 2026-11-04

Every figure here comes from a recording of Verge's wire. Point at, tap or tab to an underlined one to see the stretch of wire it comes from.

SWD

One read of the target's flash: the request, the turnaround and acknowledge, and 32 bits of data and parity. Asked for 50 MHz, Verge sends each request at 50 MHz and reads data at 37.5 MHz.

Verge (GCC): 64 KiB flash reads at 50, 37.5, 30 and 10 MHz928 ms; 2 channels: SWCLK, SWDIO; 412 KiB
JTAG

Asked for 30 or 50 MHz, Verge's TCK runs at 30 MHz in its fast bursts: 85% of their periods are 33 ns.

Verge (GCC): probe-rs JTAG throughput10.8 s; 4 channels: TCK, TMS, TDI, TDO; 4.3 MiB
cJTAG

Every JTAG bit is a frame of packets on TMSC. In OScan1 a frame has three: inverted TDI, TMS and TDO. With packets at 50 MHz, a frame takes 127 ns, a JTAG clock of 7.9 MHz. In OScan5 a bit in a shift takes two packets, 100 ns. The TDO packet is the long one: the probe waits out the target's TDO delay before ending it.

Verge (GCC): probe-rs cJTAG OScan1 throughput29.5 s; 2 of 3 channels: TCKC, TMSC; 11 MiBVerge (GCC): probe-rs cJTAG OScan5 throughput22.3 s; 2 of 3 channels: TCKC, TMSC; 9.5 MiB
