We build Verge, open firmware for NXP's MCU-Link and MCU-Link Pro debug probes, and the tools around it: verge installs it, a bench measures it against the firmware NXP ships, and tickwave shows the bench's wire recordings.

Verge

for NXP's MCU-Link and MCU-Link Pro

A probe's firmware decides how fast it talks to a target, which protocols it speaks, and how reliably it reads what comes back.

Verge generates the SWD and JTAG transfer code at run time for the clock you ask for. At a fast clock it calibrates read sampling against your target when it attaches. One image runs on both probes.

Wires
SWD, JTAG, and cJTAG in OScan1, 2, 5 and 6
Trace
SWO, and serial consoles
On the Pro
Target supply, energy capture and DIO
Hosts
Any CMSIS-DAP host; probe-rs for cJTAG

tickwave

Logic and mixed-signal captures, in a web page

tickwave draws the charts on Verge's page and the measurements page. On the measurements page it also opens the recordings the wire figures come from, with every edge the analyser caught on the debug lines.

It opens your own recordings too. Its converter turns a Saleae Logic 2 binary export, a sigrok session or a VCD file into a recording, and nothing you open is uploaded.
